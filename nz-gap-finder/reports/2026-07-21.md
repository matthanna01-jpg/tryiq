# NZ Gap Finder — Digest for 2026-07-21

First run. See `../METHODOLOGY.md` for scoring rubric and sources.

## Top candidates this week

### 1. Health-admin tooling for short-staffed clinics — Confidence: High
Cross-validated across two independent categories: healthcare sits on
Immigration NZ's Green List as a critical shortage sector (nurses, medical
specialists), *and* "health admin" software is independently flagged as a
strong pilot opportunity in NZ startup-ecosystem commentary. When a workforce
shortage and a software-gap signal point the same direction, that's a
stronger case than either alone.
- Demand 3 / Supply gap 2 / Feasibility 2
- Angle: NZ-specific practice-management or admin-automation tooling that
  reduces the load a stretched clinical workforce carries, rather than
  competing head-on with hiring more clinicians.

### 2. Owner-operator trades businesses (electrician, plumber, HVAC, builder) — Confidence: High
Trades appear on the Green List as a sustained shortage *and* are called out
directly in startup-opportunity commentary as generating "strong revenue with
steady demand" for qualified people who go out on their own — compounded by
the government's own $104B infrastructure gap and housing-supply push
needing more hands, not less.
- Demand 3 / Supply gap 3 / Feasibility 3
- Angle: lowest-barrier entry on this list — an existing trade qualification
  plus business setup, not a product to build from scratch.

### 3. Startup back-office-as-a-service (fractional finance ops, compliance, legal templates) — Confidence: Medium
Called out directly by NZ startup-ecosystem sources as a gap: early founders
need part-time help with finance ops, compliance, sales systems, and
NZ-specific legal templates (data rights, employment agreements, licensing)
that don't require building a product, just a service business.
- Demand 2 / Supply gap 2 / Feasibility 3
- Angle: service business riding NZ's genuine 2026 startup growth (~$389M
  raised across 23 rounds YTD) without needing to be a startup itself.

### 4. Export-first software in AgTech, logistics, marine, and enterprise/industrial niches — Confidence: Medium
Flagged as strong-potential categories precisely *because* they don't depend
on NZ's small domestic market — built for NZ conditions/expertise (e.g.
agriculture, marine) but sold to global customers from day one, which is how
NZ's software winners typically scale.
- Demand 2 / Supply gap 2 / Feasibility 2
- Angle: needs domain expertise to pick a specific wedge; too broad as
  stated to act on directly yet.

### 5. Municipal infrastructure financing / govtech — Confidence: Low (large, slow-moving)
Treasury/Infrastructure Commission cite a $104B infrastructure gap ($83B
public investment shortfall, $21B to eliminate the current housing
shortage), and a June 2026 think-tank report pins the housing crisis on
council inability to fund pipes and roads for new subdivisions.
- Demand 3 / Supply gap 3 / Feasibility 1
- Angle: real and large, but this is a policy-and-capital problem, not one a
  small operator or startup can address directly — included for completeness,
  not as an actionable pick.

## Weak-evidence / needs deeper data next run

- **Specific import-reliant product categories** (e.g. "NZ imports X, nobody
  makes it locally"): general trade data shows imports up 26% YoY led by
  petroleum and electrical machinery, but that's macro, not niche-level. Needs
  Stats NZ Infoshare line-item data to do properly — flagged as a
  methodology gap, see `METHODOLOGY.md`.
- **Consumer-forum complaint signals** ("why doesn't NZ have X"): search
  didn't surface concrete recurring threads this run. Worth a more targeted
  pass (e.g. specific subreddit search, Trade Me category gaps) next time.
